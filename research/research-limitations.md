# Research limitations and open questions

## Source bias

- **Platform-published content dominates.** 16 of the 24 story records come from the Selar blog. These are promotional case studies by a creator-commerce platform. They select for success, frame outcomes around the platform, and rarely report costs. They were used for **patterns** (what creators do and struggle with), not for benchmarks.
- **Survivorship bias.** Success stories are over-represented. Failure and setback material is limited to a few accounts (Pasheda, Passive Income Anna's early losses, Felix Ohaeri's earlier ventures, OkadaBooks' closure, Oo Nwoye's Callbase).
- **Industry statistics were mostly secondary.** The *Nigeria Creator Economy Report 2025* figures (share of creators under $100 a month and so on) were seen only through search summaries of TechCabal coverage (403 on direct access). The NECLive/Frontyard 2026 report was seen only through its announcement. Neither primary report was read.

## Self-reported and inconsistent figures

- Nearly all income and audience figures are **self-reported** and unverified (marked `selfReported: true`).
- Some figures are internally inconsistent: Passive Income Anna's headline says ₦200m while the body confirms ₦100m; Ransford's "$5,000 a month" headline versus about $5,000 over six weeks in the body; OkadaBooks' reach figures differ by source; Selar's 2025 payout figures differ by publication.
- Currencies are sometimes unstated ("seven figures", "over a million a month").
- **No figure from the research is used as a game balance value.** The game uses its own configurable, deliberately game-like numbers.

## Coverage gaps

- **Podcasters:** the TechCabal podcasting article returned 403; only search summaries were seen (sponsorship and live events as main routes). No first-person Nigerian podcaster story was captured.
- **Musicians and artists:** only aggregate Spotify royalty data (Condia, partly paywalled); no individual direct-to-fan story was captured.
- **Student freelancers:** search results were generic blogs or unverifiable composites. No verified first-person student freelancer story was found (Olamide Femi-Taiwo and the Social Media Oga started young but are not clearly students in their sources).
- **Geography:** most sources don't state a city or state. Verified locations are Lagos, Delta (origin), Kaduna (workshops) and Calabar (Cross River). Northern, eastern and rural creators are under-represented. No regional representation was invented.
- **Gender and age** were not systematically recorded.
- **Initial lead unavailable:** the Big Cabal "how to make money from YouTube content as a Nigerian" link (a development host) returned 503.

## Interpretation risks

- Mapping qualitative patterns to numbers (conversion rates, churn and so on) is a design choice, not a measurement.
- Infrastructure events (power, network) reflect commonly reported constraints and the NECLive/Frontyard finding, but their frequency in the game is tuned for fun and fairness, not realism.
- The Ghanaian comparator (Ransford) is excluded from Nigerian counts.

## Open questions for future research

1. Typical costs (tools, ads, data, power) for Nigerian digital-product creators, to sanity-check expense ratios.
2. Real conversion and refund rates on African creator checkouts (would need platform data or surveys).
3. Membership retention patterns for paid WhatsApp/Telegram communities.
4. First-person podcaster and musician monetisation stories from reputable outlets.
5. Regional differences (north, east, rural) in creator constraints and channels.
6. Read the full NECLive/Frontyard 2026 report and the Nigeria Creator Economy Report 2025 primary sources.
